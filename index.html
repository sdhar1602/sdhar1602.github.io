Research Fellow · Stanford GSB

Soumyadeep Dhar.

Operations, Information & Technology

I am currently a Research Fellow in Operations, Information, and Technology (OIT), at the Stanford Graduate School of Business, where I am fortunate to be advised by Prof. Ömer Karaduman and Prof. Fabian Nagel. I graduated from the Indian Institute of Technology (IIT) Kharagpur with an integrated Bachelors and Masters in Industrial and Systems Engineering.

My academic interests lie primarily in Energy, with a particular focus on electricity markets, demand forecasting and sustainability. On the AI side, my work centers on interpretability, with a particular emphasis on distillation. More broadly, I work on data-driven modeling to understand complex systems and inform decision-making.

I am always open to collaborating on intriguing projects or exchanging ideas, whether they are well-formed research directions or early exploratory thoughts. Please feel free to reach out if you are interested in any overlap or simply want to share your thoughts. If, by the end, we are both a little more confused than when we started, that usually means the conversation was worthwhile; such is the joy of research!

Featured paper · Applied Energy, 2026

Forecasting hourly demand years ahead from GDP and invariant load profiles

The method tests whether the hourly shape of demand stays statistically invariant, projects annual demand from GDP, then rebuilds the hourly curve. Every number below comes from the paper: Singapore 2004–2022, plus six European grids.

ScienceDirect
  1. Seasonality indices 19 years

    Half-hourly system demand from Singapore’s Energy Market Authority, decomposed into month, weekday and hour indices.

  2. Invariance test 4.24%

    Average largest deviation of the hourly indices across years, from pairwise one-tailed t-tests.

  3. GDP regression r = 0.995

    Annual demand tracks GDP; linear models reach an R² of 0.945 to 0.980.

  4. Hourly forecast ≤ 6.87%

    Worst hourly MAPE of the five-year-ahead forecasts for 2017–2022.

Singapore · five-year-ahead forecasts, 2017–2022
  • Hourly MAPE
  • Annual-total error

Five years ahead. Each year is forecast five years in advance: a GDP regression gives the annual total, and the invariant indices spread it across the hours of the year. Hourly MAPE stays at or below 6.87%, even through the COVID-19 shock of 2020 and 2021.

Six European grids · two-year-ahead hourly forecasts

Where GDP still drives demand. Wherever GDP and electricity demand stay coupled (r > 0.8), hourly MAPE stays under 8%. Accuracy degrades in economies where the two have decoupled (Germany 10.14%, United Kingdom 11.63%), which marks the boundary of the method.

Day-ahead extension · mean yearly MAPE, 2018–2022
  • Exponential smoothing with invariant indices
  • Benchmarks

A short-term bonus. Holding the seasonal factors at the invariant indices leaves a triple-seasonal exponential-smoothing model with only two parameters (α = 0.19, β = 0.88). Its mean day-ahead MAPE across 2018–2022 is the lowest of the seven methods compared.
